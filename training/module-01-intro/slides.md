# Slide 2 — Learning objectives

- Identify practical use cases and limitations

# Slide 3 — What is Generative AI?
Generative AI refers to models that can create text, images, code, or other media from prompts. Examples: large language models (text), diffusion/image models (images).

# Slide 4 — How it works (brief)
- Models are trained on large datasets to predict tokens
- They generalize patterns and can generate novel outputs
- Not deterministic; outputs may vary per run

# Slide 5 — Common use cases
- Content generation (articles, summaries)
- Code assistance and templates
- Creative exploration: images, stories, ideation

# Slide 6 — Limitations & Risks
- Hallucinations (confident but incorrect assertions)
- Biases in training data
- Privacy concerns when using sensitive prompts
- Cost and compute considerations

# Slide 7 — Safety & Responsible Use
- Validate model outputs before publishing
- Redact or avoid sharing sensitive data in prompts
- Consider human review for critical outputs

# Slide 8 — Prompt design tips
- Be specific about task, format, length, and tone
- Provide examples when helpful (few-shot)
- Ask for step-by-step or bullet lists for structured output

# Slide 9 — Example prompts
- "Write a 50-word professional bio for a software engineer who specializes in accessibility." 
- "Summarize the following paragraph in one sentence: <paste text>"
- "List 5 ideas for beginner blog posts about generative AI, in bullet points."
